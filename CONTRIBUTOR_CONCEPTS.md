# Contributor concepts

Status: **EXPLORATORY. No named person is involved, has been approached, or has endorsed this project.** Do not write material attributed to, or "in the voice of", any real person, and never invent quotations.

---

## Working examples from the brief

| Working concept | Inspired by | Natural subject home |
|---|---|---|
| "The Book of Ricky" | Ricky Gervais: humour, scepticism, irreverence | Laughter; Doubt |
| "The Book of Stephen" | Stephen Fry: curiosity, eloquence, humanism | Words; Wonder; Stories (myth) |
| "The Book of Richard" | Richard Dawkins: evolution, natural selection | Kin; Beginnings |

## Advantages of person-associated books

- **Distinct voices.** This is the strongest protection against a single generic voice.
- **Credibility and reach.** Known names bring readers and press.
- **Precedent.** Many Bible books carry personal names; anthologies are normal.
- **Responsibility.** A named author owns their words, which fits "no unquestionable authority".

## Difficulties

1. **Permission and practicality.** Busy public figures may decline, delay, or contribute something short. A table of contents built around them is hostage to their availability.
2. **Reputation risk.** Living people's reputations change, and contributors may say or do things later that readers find objectionable. If a book is *named* after them, the whole collection inherits that controversy and cannot easily detach. Dawkins in particular is a polarising figure, including among atheists. This is noted as a factual description of public reception, not a judgement.
3. **Dating.** First names that are instantly recognisable now ("Ricky", "Stephen") may mean little in 50 years or in other countries. "Richard" and "Stephen" are also ambiguous.
4. **Referencing.** "Ricky 4:3" reads as a joke; "Stephen 2:1" is ambiguous.
5. **Cult of personality.** Naming books after celebrity atheists risks making The Teryn look like a fan anthology of the "New Atheist" era, which undercuts "a book about life, not gods" and the broad international audience.
6. **Balance.** All three working examples are British, male, white and of a similar generation. That is a strong signal to address early, given the aim of avoiding Western assumptions.
7. **Editorial control.** Famous contributors may resist editing. That needs agreeing up front.

## Status of the idea (28 Oct 2026)

Books named after famous atheists were an off-the-cuff example in the brief, not a settled wish. **Parked; open either way.** If revisited, there are three ways to fill such a book: written *by* the person (needs their involvement), written *about* them (care needed over implied endorsement), or built from a historical figure's own verified, public-domain words.

## One possible approach (proposal)

**Subject-named books, with credited contributors, plus a dedicated Voices part.**

- **Books are named by subject; authors are credited.** For example: *The Book of Laughter*, by [contributor]. The book outlives any one association, and a subject book can have one author, several, or be editorial.
- **The Letters (Part 6).** Shorter contributed pieces, modelled on the epistles: *A Letter on Wonder*, from [contributor]. This is a low-commitment ask for busy people, and it scales: many voices without restructuring.
- **The Book of Elders.** The only place historical people's *actual, verified words* form the text. Here person-association is safe, because the record is fixed and public domain (with care over translations).
- **Parallel accounts.** Two contributors on one subject, deliberately different, like the four gospels.

Under this model, the three working examples become *subject books we might one day invite someone to write*, not books defined by a person. For example: Laughter (an irreverent comic voice), Wonder or Words (an eloquent humanist voice), Kin (an evolutionary biologist).

## Contributor pool: dimensions to diversify (not names yet)

- Discipline: scientists, philosophers, novelists, poets, comedians, doctors, historians, palliative-care workers, teachers, engineers.
- Geography and culture: Africa, Asia, Latin America, the Middle East, Indigenous perspectives, not only the UK and US.
- Gender, age and generation.
- Lived experience: people who left religions; people from secular-by-default societies.
- Fame: a mix of well-known and unknown. Testimonies need no fame at all.

## Open questions

- Do we draft placeholder books ourselves first, then invite contributors? Or approach contributors early and design around responses?
- What editorial rights does The Teryn keep (style, verse numbering, fact-checking, rejection)?
- Rights model: contributor keeps copyright and licenses to The Teryn, or assigns it? Is there an open licence option?
- How are disagreements between contributors shown: suppressed, footnoted, or published as parallel accounts?
