# Anagram book names: trial list

Status: **EXPLORATORY** (11 Nov 2026). The idea: each book's name is an anagram of a great atheist's name, ideally matching the book's subject, with a plain subtitle so readers can find what they need, e.g. **Gabath** — *The Book of Courage*.

**Rule (founder, 11 Nov):** strict. Only people who clearly described themselves as atheists. Self-described agnostics (e.g. Darwin, Sagan) and people who rejected the label (e.g. Einstein) are excluded.

**Verification:** every person below is believed to meet the rule, but **each needs a sourced quotation** of them calling themselves an atheist before use. The "Evidence to find" column says what to look for.

---

## Strongest candidates

| Name | Anagram of | Person | Suggested book | Evidence to find |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Gabath** | Bhagat (Singh) | Indian revolutionary, executed in 1931 aged 23 | Courage | His essay *Why I Am an Atheist* (1930) |
| **Amagoras** | Saramago (José) | Portuguese novelist, Nobel Prize 1998 | Makers, or Words | His public statements calling himself an atheist |
| **Simeon** | Simone (de Beauvoir) | French philosopher and writer | Ageing (she wrote *Old Age*, 1970) | Her memoirs on losing faith and her atheism |
| **Kathar** | Akhtar (Javed) | Indian poet and lyricist | Words, or Makers | His public statements; Richard Dawkins Award 2020 |

## Possible, with problems

| Name | Anagram of | Person | Suggested book | Problem |
|---|---|---|---|---|
| **Eros** | Rose (Ernestine) | Polish-born campaigner for women's rights and abolition in the US; gave *A Defence of Atheism* (1861) | Love, or Justice | Eros is the Greek god of love: a god's name hiding an atheist. Clever, but may read as a joke. Her life suits Justice better than Love. |
| **Terras** / **Rarest** | Sartre (Jean-Paul) | French philosopher of freedom and responsibility | Responsibility | Perfect subject match, but neither anagram is strong. |
| **Damas** | Adams (Douglas) | British author; called himself a "radical atheist" | Wonder | The anagram is weak. |

## Notes

- **Biblical echoes.** "Simeon" is a Bible name. In Luke 2, Simeon is an old man ready to die in peace, which happens to suit Ageing. "Gabath" resembles Gabbatha (John 19:13). "Kathar" resembles the Cathars, a medieval Christian sect. These echoes give a scriptural sound, but each should be a deliberate choice.
- **Real words versus invented names.** Real-word anagrams (e.g. *Inward* from Darwin) are rare. Invented names like Gabath and Amagoras sound most like scripture.
- **Spread.** This list so far covers India ×2, Portugal, France ×2, Poland/US and the UK, with two women. Keep widening it.
- **Surnames or first names.** Whichever gives the better anagram (e.g. Simone, not Beauvoir).
