## A. C. Grayling, *The Good Book* (2011)

- Published as *The Good Book: A Secular Bible* (UK, Bloomsbury) and *The Good Book: A Humanist Bible* (US, Walker, 597 pp.).
- **Structure:** an "Epistle to the Reader", then 14 books:
  - Genesis, Wisdom, Parables, Concord, Lamentations, Consolations, Sages
  - Songs, Histories, Proverbs, The Lawgiver, Acts, Epistles, The Good
- Short chapters with numbered verses, cited Bible-style (e.g. *The Good 8:11*).
- **Method:** a single editor distilled and adapted material from hundreds of authors and more than a thousand texts, largely classical and Western.
- **Style:** deliberately scripture-like. Many verses begin "And…", and it uses an elevated, archaic-leaning cadence. For example, Genesis 1 retells Newton and the apple in a biblical register.

### Implications for The Teryn

The Teryn should be clearly distinct from Grayling's book in at least these ways:

1. **Original writing and many voices**, not one editor's adaptation of older texts.
2. **Modern plain English**, not scriptural pastiche. The Grayling sample is a useful warning about what verse-numbered prose sounds like when the cadence takes over.
3. **Science-forward and current.** It tells the real origin story and records what is still unknown.
4. **Global sources.** Grayling draws mainly on Western classical material.
5. **A living, versioned collection**, not a single fixed edition.
