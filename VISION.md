# Vision

Status: **Starting brief, open to revision.** Summarised from the project master prompt (28 Oct 2026).

## In one line

**The Teryn is a book about life, not gods.**

## Founding line

### The shape of the premise (agreed 11 Nov 2026)

The entire premise is that there are no gods. Said bluntly at the start, it is confrontational and would turn readers away immediately. So:

- **The cover** carries an honest, gentle signal: the tagline "a book about life, not gods". Readers are never misled about what kind of book this is.
- **The opening** of The Teryn is the founding line without its first sentence (see candidates below).
- **Throughout**, the idea is carried gently, as in "Every life is in human hands, and human hands are enough."
- **The very last line of the whole Teryn** is: **"There are no gods."** By then it confirms what the reader has already seen, rather than confronting them. It is used once only; chapters do not end with it.

### Opening line candidates

Both kept open. The founder currently prefers **A**:

> **A.** We have this universe, this planet, this life and each other. What we make of them is our responsibility.

> **B.** We have one extraordinary opportunity to exist. The Teryn is about what we do with it.

## Central theme

Agreed 11 Nov 2026:

**You are responsible for your own life and actions. No one else, and nothing else, holds that for you.**

**The theme depends on there being no gods.** A believer can say "I have free will too, so what's the difference?" The difference is that, with no gods:

- there is no plan written in advance;
- no final judge, and no one above us keeping score;
- no one to forgive us except the people we have harmed;
- no one coming to put the world right if we don't.

Responsibility does not stop with us at the moment of choosing; it stops with us *ultimately*. That is not a burden; it is what makes a life your own.

The theme is also bigger than religion. It covers every way of handing a life over: fate, "meant to be", the stars, "only following orders", "that's just how I am".

Honesty note: The Teryn says "your choices are yours". It does not claim that science has settled the philosophical question of free will.

## What it is

A secular collection organised like a Bible, into books, chapters and numbered verses. It brings together human knowledge, wisdom, experience, philosophy and ideas about existence: what it means to be alive, what we know about our world, how to treat one another, how we find meaning, and how humanity might build a better future.

It is a **library of human understanding contained within one work**, and a **celebration of existence**. It is about everything we *do* have: life, love, knowledge, humour, beauty, curiosity, friendship, discovery, responsibility, freedom, the natural world, each other.

## Ambition

- A recognisable, culturally significant secular work.
- Read throughout a lifetime; quoted and discussed.
- Spanning science, philosophy, morality, human experience, history and practical wisdom.
- Accessible across countries, cultures, backgrounds and generations: readable by an intelligent teenager, rewarding for an educated adult.
- Able to grow and evolve as understanding develops.
- Inspiring without supernatural promises or unquestionable authority.

## Stance on religion

Revised 11 Nov 2026: **The premise that there are no gods runs through the whole collection, carried gently. It is stated bluntly only once, as the last line of the book (see "The shape of the premise" above). The Teryn never argues, mocks or insults.**

- **Carrying** the idea gently ("Every life is in human hands, and human hands are enough") is the norm.
- **Arguing**, **mocking** or **belittling** believers ("only a fool would…") is never acceptable.

It should be a book a religious reader can read without feeling attacked. Its honesty, confidence and kindness do the persuading.

(Supersedes earlier wording of 28 Oct and 11 Nov 2026.)

It is not a religion, not a rebuttal of the Bible, and not a new dogma.

## What it is not

- A popular science book.
- A textbook on atheism.
- A point-by-point rebuttal of the Bible.
- Anti-religious jokes.
- A political manifesto.
- Cliché self-help.
- A substitute religion.
- An encyclopaedia.
- A claim to ultimate truth.
