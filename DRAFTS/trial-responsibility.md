# Trial chapter: Responsibility

Status: **TRIAL DRAFT, NOT APPROVED TEXT.** Written 11 Nov 2026 to test the agreed style over a whole chapter:
- short verses, one per line, that read straight through;
- "state it, don't argue it";
- serious but warm, with no jokes;
- the central theme of responsibility.

No book is assigned yet.

---

1. There are no gods.
2. No plan was written for you before you were born.
3. No one is keeping score, and no one is coming to fix what we break.
4. What we do is ours.
5. It is comforting to think it was all decided long ago, that someone else holds the plan and we only have to follow.
6. People have handed their lives to many things: to gods and to fate, to the stars, to "meant to be", to whoever was giving the orders.
7. Some hand their lives to themselves, and say, "That's just how I am."
8. But no one wrote your life before you lived it.
9. There is no script, and no one waiting in the wings to give you your lines.
10. Every good thing you have ever seen was done by someone who chose to do it.
11. So was every cruelty.
12. The kindness and the harm, the courage and the excuses, belong to the people who chose them.
13. This does not mean everything is in your hands.
14. You did not choose where you were born, or to whom, or the body you were given.
15. Much of a life is luck, good and bad, and no one earns their starting place.
16. What is yours is what you do next.
17. It is enough to change a day, a friendship, a life.
18. If you do harm, there is no one above to forgive you.
19. Forgiveness belongs to the people you hurt, and you will have to ask them for it.
20. If you do good, no one is keeping a record to reward you later.
21. The reward is the good itself, and the people it reaches.
22. This can feel like a heavy thing to carry.
23. It is lighter when you remember you do not carry it alone.
24. We are responsible for ourselves, and to each other.
25. No one is coming to save us, so we look after each other.
26. That is not a burden.
27. It is the whole point.

---

## Notes for discussion

- **Shape:**
  - 1–4: the plain statement.
  - 5–7: the ways people hand their lives over.
  - 8–12: no script; choices belong to those who make them.
  - 13–17: honesty about luck.
  - 18–21: life without a final judge.
  - 22–27: we are not alone, and that is the point.
- **Verses 13–17 (luck)** are there so the theme is honest and kind. Without them, "you are responsible for everything" can sound like blaming people for illness, poverty or bad luck. Do they soften the theme too much, or make it stronger?
- **Verse 1** opens bluntly. Is that right for the start of a chapter, or should "There are no gods" arrive a little later, once the reader is listening?
- **Verses 18–19** are probably the sharpest expression of the no-gods difference: there is no forgiveness from above, only from the people you hurt.
- **Reference test:** if this were chapter 1 of a book called *Responsibility*, verse 4 would be cited as *Responsibility 1:4*.
