# Principles

Status: **Starting brief, open to revision.** Taken from the master prompt. My suggested additions are marked **[PROPOSED]**.

## Intellectual principles

1. **Reality matters.** Claims about the physical world must match the best available evidence.
2. **Knowledge is provisional.** The collection must be able to change as understanding changes.
3. **No unquestionable authority.** No contributor, editor or passage is beyond criticism.
4. **Humanity is responsible** for its behaviour, societies, environment and future.
5. **Morality is a human undertaking**, explored through reason, empathy, consequences, cooperation and experience.
6. **Uncertainty is acceptable.** Where we do not know, we say so.
7. **Wonder matters.** A natural explanation does not diminish beauty or significance.
8. **Different perspectives matter**, without giving unsupported claims equal standing with evidence.
9. Atheism is the starting philosophical position, **not permission to overstate what science establishes** about metaphysical questions.

## Style principles

Aim for: **clear, intelligent, memorable, accessible, occasionally profound.**

Avoid:

- Archaic biblical English.
- Excessive solemnity.
- Preaching.
- Empty slogans.
- Pretentious language.
- Needless jargon.
- Repeated arguments against religion.
- Treating science as a source of moral commandments.
- False certainty.
- Forcing every sentence into a proverb.

Make room for disagreement, ambiguity, joy, anger, grief, curiosity and wonder. (The brief originally included humour; as of 11 Nov 2026 the writing should not aim to be funny. See `DECISIONS.md`.)

## Research principles

- Use reliable sources; prefer primary research and authoritative references.
- Verify every quotation and attribution. **Never invent citations.**
- Distinguish evidence from interpretation; identify disputed questions.
- Record sources so material can be fact-checked; flag content likely to date.
- Take special care with history, science, religion and named individuals.
- Eloquence is not accuracy.

## Agreed additions

- **Carry it gently; never argue it** (revised 11 Nov 2026; supersedes earlier versions). The premise that there are no gods runs through the collection, carried gently, above all through the theme of responsibility. The blunt statement "There are no gods" appears once only, as the very last line of The Teryn. It never argues, mocks or belittles believers, and pokes fun at no one. Test: would a thoughtful religious reader feel *disagreed with* (fine) or *looked down on* (not fine)?

## Proposed additions
- **[PROPOSED] Write for the world.** Draw examples, sayings and history from many cultures by default, not as an afterthought.
- **[PROPOSED] Show confidence levels.** In factual books, distinguish well-established findings, current best explanations and open questions, in the text or the notes.
- **[PROPOSED] Never put words in real mouths.** No invented or "in the style of" material attributed to real people.
