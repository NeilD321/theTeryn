# Ideas backlog

Status: **BRAINSTORM. Nothing here is approved.** Books are grouped under the *proposed* Parts in `ARCHITECTURE.md`. Names are working titles.

Key: ★ = suggested for a First Edition core (proposal only). ◆ = unconventional idea.

---

## Opening

- **The Opening** ★: one short chapter built around the founding line (see `VISION.md`). It also covers how to read The Teryn and how it changes. It is welcoming and argues nothing.

## Part 1 — Origins

- **The Book of Beginnings** ★: the real origin story, from the earliest universe to the first life.
  - Told as narrative, with wonder. Explicit about where knowledge stops (what, if anything, came "before"; how life began).
  - Style: plain epic narrative.
  - Risk: fast-changing cosmology; flag claims by confidence.
- **The Book of Earth**: deep time, plate tectonics, atmosphere, ice ages, mass extinctions. Style: chronicle, with a sense of scale.
- **The Book of Kin** ★◆: evolution reframed as **kinship**. Every living thing is a distant relative.
  - Covers the tree of life, natural selection, other species.
  - Style: explanatory, warm. A natural home for Dawkins-style subject matter, written by us or a contributor.
- **The Book of Ancestors**: human evolution, other human species, migration across the planet, the deep human family. Style: family history.
- **The Book of Mind**: brains, consciousness, intelligence (human and animal), the self. Style: exploratory, question-heavy. Could sit in Knowing.

## Part 2 — Knowing

- **The Book of Doubt** ★: evidence, reason, how to change your mind, cognitive biases, the joy of being wrong. Style: conversational, partly in dialogue.
- **The Book of Errors** ◆: humanity's great mistakes and how we noticed them.
  - Examples: an Earth-centred universe, disease from "bad air", bloodletting, and science's own moral failures such as scientific racism and eugenics.
  - Style: chronicle. It shows that knowledge is provisional, and protects The Teryn from treating science as infallible.
- **The Book of Discovery**: true stories of how particular things were found out, used the way parables are. Style: short stories.
  - Risk: many famous science anecdotes are embellished or myths. Strict fact-checking required.
- **The Book of the Unknown** ★◆: a scripture with a whole book about what it does not know.
  - Covers dark matter, the origin of life, consciousness, why there is something rather than nothing, and what we cannot currently answer.
  - Style: contemplative, often in questions.
  - Possibly The Teryn's most distinctive statement against dogma.
- **The Book of Stories** (working title: Gods, Myths): religion and myth as human phenomena, treated with curiosity and respect.
  - Covers why humans make gods, what myths do, and what religion has given and cost us.
  - Style: anthropological, generous.
  - Risk: sliding into rebuttal. Needs firm editorial guardrails.
- **The Book of Words**: language, storytelling, translation, how words shape thought.

## Part 3 — Being Human (ordered as a life)

- **The Book of Arrival** ◆: birth and childhood. Possibly written *to* a child, as letters to someone newly born.
- **The Book of Questions** ◆: real questions asked by children, answered honestly ("Where was I before I was born?", "Will you die?"). Questions would need to be gathered ethically, with consent. Style: Q&A, dialogue.
- **The Book of Love** ★: love, sex, desire, partnership, heartbreak. Style: frank and poetic. The Song of Songs is precedent for erotic poetry in scripture.
- **The Book of Friendship**: chosen family, loyalty, loneliness.
- **The Book of Work** ◆: craft, making, vocation, drudgery, rest. Missing from the original subject list, yet it fills much of most lives.
- **The Book of the Body** ◆: senses, food, pleasure, health, illness, disability, sleep. Missing from the original list.
- **The Book of Laughter**: play, delight and why we laugh, treated as a part of human life. Since 11 Nov 2026 the writing is not meant to be funny, so this book is in question.
- **The Book of Days** ◆: ordinary life (mornings, meals, weather, small joys), as short daily readings. Possibly 365 entries, giving The Teryn a built-in daily reading plan.
- **The Book of Sorrow** ★: suffering, grief, depression, the unfairness of pain.
  - A secular counterpart to Job and Lamentations: suffering is not a test or a punishment, and no explanation is owed. Only presence, honesty and comfort.
  - Style: lament, poetry, testimony.
- **The Book of Ageing**: growing older, memory, change, what the old know.
- **The Book of Endings** ★: dying, death and mortality.
  - What death is biologically. Ancient arguments for not fearing death (Epicurus, Lucretius; verify texts). How we mourn and how we are remembered.
  - Style: contemplative, consoling, unflinching.

## Part 4 — Living Well

- **The Book of Conduct** ★: the foundations of ethics without commandments: empathy, reciprocity, honesty, fairness, responsibility.
  - Style: mixed sayings and short reflections. The closest analogue to Proverbs.
  - Note: reciprocity principles appear independently across cultures (e.g. Confucius, *Analects* 15:24). Verify all citations.
- **The Book of Dilemmas** ◆: hard moral cases where The Teryn **does not give the answer**.
  - Several positions are argued well; the reader decides.
  - Style: case studies and dialogue. It embodies "no unquestionable authority".
- **The Book of Sayings**: verified wisdom from many cultures and eras, attributed or traditional. Style: aphorism. A direct answer to Western bias. Very heavy on verification.
- **The Book of Mistakes**: personal failure, apology, forgiveness, starting again. Could merge into Conduct.
- **The Book of Wonder** ★: attention, awe, beauty, joy. Why a natural explanation adds to wonder rather than removing it. Style: poetic, observational.
- **The Book of Courage**: fear, resilience, standing up. Could merge into Conduct.

## Part 5 — Together

- **The Book of Strangers** ◆: cooperation, and how the moral circle widened from family to tribe to nation to species. Why we help people we will never meet.
- **The Book of Chronicles**: a compressed, genuinely global human history, focused on turning points, cooperation and catastrophe. Style: chronicle.
- **The Book of Power**: politics, power, law, rights, and how power is checked.
  - Must stay non-partisan. It describes principles and history, not policy.
- **The Book of Justice**: fairness, inequality, poverty, human rights.
- **The Book of War and Peace**: violence, war, peacemaking.
- **The Book of Makers**: art, music, literature, why humans make beautiful useless things.
- **The Book of Home** ◆: belonging, place, land, migration, exile.
- **The Book of Creatures** ◆: our relationships with and obligations to other animals. Could sit in Origins or Living Well.

## Part 6 — Voices

- **The Letters** ★ (structure to be confirmed): contributed pieces addressed to the reader, modelled on the epistles, e.g. *A Letter on Doubt*, from [contributor]. Allows famous and lesser-known voices without giving them a whole book each.
- **The Book of Elders** ◆: verified words of historical thinkers who lived without gods, or questioned them, from many cultures.
  - Candidates to research: Epicurus, Lucretius, Wang Chong (China, 1st century CE), al-Ma'arri (Syria, 11th century), Hume, George Eliot, Ingersoll.
  - Each needs careful checking. Several were not "atheists" in the modern sense, and the Cārvāka (Indian materialist) texts survive mostly through their opponents.
  - Public-domain translations, or our own, are needed.
- **The Book of Testimonies** ◆: ordinary people's first-hand accounts: facing a death, leaving a faith, finding purpose, surviving. Gathered by interview, with consent and privacy safeguards. It democratises the collection.

## Part 7 — Tomorrow

- **The Book of Inheritance** ★: what we owe those not yet born: environment, climate, institutions, knowledge.
- **The Book of Machines**: technology, artificial intelligence, biotechnology. Will date fast; versioned heavily.
- **The Book of Tomorrow** ★: the long future (space, the survival of humanity, the far future of the universe), ending on what we do with the time we have. The Teryn's "Revelation": an honest ending that is open, not prophesied.

## Missing areas worth considering (from the brief's list)

- Work, the body, food, sleep and play (now proposed above).
- Money and economics, as a human invention.
- Disability and neurodiversity, so the "human experience" is not written for one kind of body or mind.
- Parenting, as distinct from childhood.
- Solitude and silence.
- Luck and chance: how much of a life is not earned.
- Ritual without religion: marking births, comings-of-age, marriages and deaths secularly. This is practical, widely wanted, and a place where The Teryn could actually be *used*.

## Draft fragments the founder liked (11 Nov 2026)

Sketches written to show a direction for the central theme. **Not approved text**, but worth keeping as touchstones for voice.

> No one wrote your life before you lived it.
> There is no script,
> and no one waiting in the wings to give you your lines.
> What you do is yours:
> the kindness and the harm,
> the courage and the excuses.
> That is not a burden.
> It is the whole point.

> It is comforting to think it was all decided long ago,
> that someone else holds the plan
> and we only have to follow.
> But every good thing you have ever seen
> was done by someone who chose to do it.

> There are no gods.
> No plan was written for you,
> no one is keeping score,
> and no one is coming to fix what we break.
> What we do is ours.

The third fragment is a model of "state it, don't argue it": plain and confident, with no insult.

## Gaps found by the needs map (11 Nov 2026)

See `RESEARCH/needs-map.md`.

- **Blessings**: words for wishing someone well (births, farewells, new starts, meals).
- **Stories**: memorable narratives, including ones for children. The current list is almost all reflection.
- **Words said together**: a short shared text a group could say aloud. Handle with care, as it may feel too much like prayer.
- **Rest**: stopping, a weekly pause, sabbath without religion.
- **Courage**: may need its own book; fear is one of the most common needs.

## Other ideas (not books)

- **Parallel accounts**: like the four gospels, some subjects get two or more contributed treatments side by side, deliberately disagreeing.
- **Marginal dissent**: an editorial convention where a printed note records a serious objection to a passage.
- **A Teryn for younger readers**, as a later edition.
- **Ceremony readings**: an index of passages suitable for funerals, weddings and namings.
